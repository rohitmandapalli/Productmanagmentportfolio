ML-assisted clinical document authoring

Cut document turnaround by 80% with ML drafting and human sign-off.

Client
Global pharmaceutical organisation
Role
Product lead. Found the need, set the vision and MVP, and briefed the ML engineers who built it on IBM Watson.
Users
4,000+ scientific, clinical and regulatory staff
  • 80%faster document turnaround
  • ~$2Mannual savings
  • 80–90%model accuracy at launch

What was done

  • Mapped the manual, email-based authoring process with medical writers and regulatory teams.
  • Set MVP scope, accuracy thresholds, confidence levels and review rules.
  • Briefed ML engineers on training data, draft logic and when to flag a result.
  • Drove adoption with demos and training in plain language.

Principle. Automate the repetitive. Flag uncertainty. Humans decide high-risk calls.

How the figures were measured

Turnaround. Manual research and writing replaced by automated drafting. The 8 hours is review and sign-off.

Savings. Hours saved across 4,000 writers and scientists, multiplied by hourly cost, including time lost to human error.

Accuracy. Machine output compared with human-written reports, section by section. It measures output quality, not ML engineering.

If rebuilt today

Hindsight, not part of the delivery: retrieval from approved sources, traceable citations, hallucination checks, source-grounding scores and reviewer-override metrics.

Paper to digital: processing time down from around 10 days to 2.

Team

AI-assisted review, access and sign-in discovery

Testing whether AI can safely widen review beyond 1 in 40 submissions.

Client
UK professional regulator
Role
Led product discovery. Supported the move into Alpha.
Scale
About 100,000 users and 200,000 submissions a year
Status
Not a live service. Discovery and Alpha only, so there are no production results to report.

AI-assisted review

  • Mapped the service, user needs and comparators. Produced the Alpha plan and a future-state blueprint.
  • Found a light-touch reflective check, not hard-evidence verification. AI is therefore judged on reflection quality, reviewer consistency, feedback quality and handling of uncertainty.
  • In Alpha: reviewer workshops, prompt packs, guardrails, and when retrieval (RAG) beats a standalone LLM.

Related discovery, same regulator

  • Access control. Reviewed staff access to regulated data, found governance gaps, recommended a role-based model.
  • Multi-factor authentication. Compared text codes, authenticator apps, GOV.UK One Login, passwordless email links and third-party sign-in on security, accessibility, cost and support impact.
